# Guitar Music Theory

> A practical, fretboard-first guide to music theory for guitar players.  
> This note serves as a **Map of Content (MOC)** – follow the links to dive deeper into each topic.

---

## 1. 🎸 Fretboard Foundation

Understanding the layout of the guitar is the first step.

- **Standard tuning:** E A D G B E (low to high)
- **Memorize the natural notes** on the low E and A strings first – everything else can be derived.
- **Octave shapes** help you find the same note across the neck.
- **Unison patterns** let you play the same pitch on different strings.

📁 _Related:_ [[Fretboard Notes]], [[CAGED System]], [[Octave Shapes]]

---

## 2. 🎵 Intervals – The Building Blocks

An **interval** is the distance between two notes. On guitar, think in terms of **frets and strings**.

| Half-Steps (Frets) | Interval Name        | Common Abbreviation |
|--------------------|----------------------|----------------------|
| 0                  | Perfect Unison       | P1                   |
| 1                  | Minor 2nd            | m2                   |
| 2                  | Major 2nd            | M2                   |
| 3                  | Minor 3rd            | m3                   |
| 4                  | Major 3rd            | M3                   |
| 5                  | Perfect 4th          | P4                   |
| 6                  | Tritone              | TT (dim5/aug4)       |
| 7                  | Perfect 5th          | P5                   |
| 8                  | Minor 6th            | m6                   |
| 9                  | Major 6th            | M6                   |
| 10                 | Minor 7th            | m7                   |
| 11                 | Major 7th            | M7                   |
| 12                 | Octave               | P8                   |

- **Learn interval shapes on adjacent strings** – they are the same in standard tuning except between the G and B strings (shift one fret up).
- Intervals build scales and chords.

📁 _Related:_ [[Interval Shapes for Guitar]], [[Ear Training with Intervals]]

## 3. 📐 Scales – The Note Palettes

A **scale** is a sequence of notes defined by a formula of intervals.

### Essential Scale Formulas (in whole/half steps or half steps)

| Scale Type       | Interval Formula (in half-steps from root) |
|------------------|--------------------------------------------|
| Major            | 2 2 1 2 2 2 1                              |
| Natural Minor    | 2 1 2 2 1 2 2                              |
| Major Pentatonic | 2 2 3 2 3                                  |
| Minor Pentatonic | 3 2 2 3 2                                  |
| Blues            | 3 2 1 1 3 2 (adds ♭5 to minor pentatonic) |

- **Learn the five CAGED positions** for each scale.
- Practice **root-note identification** in every shape.
- Connect positions horizontally along the neck.

📁 _Related:_ [[Major Scale]], [[Minor Scale]], [[Pentatonic Scales]], [[Blues Scale]], [[CAGED Scale Positions]]

## 4. 🎹 Chords – Stacked Intervals

Chords are built by **stacking thirds** (every other note) from a scale.

### [[Triads]] (3-note chords)

- **[[Major]]:** Root – Major 3rd – Perfect 5th (R-3-5)
- **[[Minor]]:** Root – Minor 3rd – Perfect 5th (R-♭3-5)
- **[[Diminished]]:** Root – Minor 3rd – Diminished 5th (R-♭3-♭5)
- **[[Augmented]]:** Root – Major 3rd – Augmented 5th (R-3-♯5)

### [[Seventh Chords]] (4-note chords)

- **Major 7:** R-3-5-7
- **Dominant 7:** R-3-5-♭7
- **Minor 7:** R-♭3-5-♭7
- **Minor 7♭5 (half-dim):** R-♭3-♭5-♭7

**On guitar:**
- Learn **moveable chord shapes** (barre chords, CAGED chord forms).
- Focus on **root on the 6th, 5th, and 4th strings**.
- Practice **triad inversions** on string sets (e.g., 1-2-3, 2-3-4).

📁 _Related:_ [[Open Chords]], [[Barre Chords]], [[Triads & Inversions]], [[Seventh Chord Shapes]], [[CAGED Chords]]

## 5. 🎼 [[Diatonic Harmony]] – Chords in a Key

When you harmonize a major scale, you get a predictable sequence of chord types.

**Key of C Major example:**

| Degree | Chord  | Roman Numeral |
|--------|--------|---------------|
| I      | Cmaj7  | Imaj7         |
| ii     | Dm7    | ii7           |
| iii    | Em7    | iii7          |
| IV     | Fmaj7  | IVmaj7        |
| V      | G7     | V7 (dominant) |
| vi     | Am7    | vi7           |
| vii°   | Bm7♭5  | viiø7         |

**Pattern for any major key:** I – ii – iii – IV – V – vi – vii°

- The **I, IV, and V** chords are major (and the V is dominant).
- The **ii, iii, and vi** are minor.
- The **vii°** is diminished.

📁 _Related:_ [[Circle of Fifths]], [[Harmonizing the Major Scale]], [[Chord Progressions]]

## 6. 🔁 Common Chord Progressions

Memorizing these progressions in multiple keys unlocks thousands of songs.

- **I – V – vi – IV**  (e.g., G – D – Em – C)
- **vi – IV – I – V**  (sad-pop progression)
- **I – IV – V** (blues / rock foundation)
- **ii – V – I** (jazz turnaround)
- **I – vi – IV – V** (50s progression)
- **12-Bar Blues:** I – I – I – I – IV – IV – I – I – V – IV – I – V

📁 _Related:_ [[12 Bar Blues]], [[Common Pop Progressions]], [[Modal Interchange]]

## 7. 🥁 Rhythm & Feel for Guitarists

Theory isn’t just notes – rhythm is equally important.

- **Time signatures:** 4/4, 3/4, 6/8
- **Note values:** whole, half, quarter, eighth, sixteenth
- **Rests & ties**
- **Strumming patterns** – think of your hand as a metronome; downstrokes on the beat, upstrokes on the “and”.
- **Syncopation & accents** – accenting off-beats creates groove.

📁 _Related:_ [[Strumming Patterns]], [[Time Signatures]], [[Rhythm Notation for Guitar]]

---

## 8. 🗺️ Applying Theory to the Fretboard

- **Soloing:** Target chord tones (root, 3rd, 5th, 7th) on strong beats.
- **Chord Melody:** Combine chords and melody by voicing the melody note as the highest pitch in your chord.
- **Transposing:** Move a shape or progression to a different root note using the same relative relationships.
- **Ear training:** Sing intervals as you play them. Associate scale degrees with emotions.

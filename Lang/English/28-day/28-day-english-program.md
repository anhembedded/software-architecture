Bro, I'll build this as a 4-week English improvement program tailored to your current weaknesses, not a generic English course.

Your schedule is two hours per day: one hour in the morning and one hour in the evening. We'll focus on everyday English, spontaneous conversation, natural expressions, storytelling, and listening. We'll also use your existing technical English as a strength rather than wasting time relearning what you already know.

One important expectation: four weeks of focused practice can produce noticeable improvements, but I won't promise that you'll reach B2 everyday English in 28 days. Our target is to build a stronger A2 foundation and move toward B1-style communication, then reassess using actual performance.

## 1.2 Error analysis

These are the specific problems your program will address.

### Problem 1: Incomplete sentence structure

Original: "I was i crazy weeken with alot of activity"

Better: "I had a crazy weekend with lots of activities."

Training target: learn subject + verb + object/complement as a reliable default structure.

### Problem 2: Inconsistent verb forms

Original: "try new guitar, new game, and ride around"

Better: "I tried out a new guitar, played a new game, and rode around."

Training target: use past simple consistently when describing completed events.

### Problem 3: Translating ideas too literally

Original: "leaving me with empty space of emotion"

Better: "It left me feeling empty."

Training target: replace word-by-word translation with complete, natural expressions.

### Problem 4: Limited idiomatic vocabulary

You couldn't accurately explain "end up," "catch up," and "slip my mind."

Training target: recognize, understand, and use common conversational expressions without translating each word.

## 1.3 What success should look like after 28 days

These are training targets, not guaranteed CEFR outcomes.

- Speak for 2–3 minutes about a familiar topic without reading a script.
- Tell a personal story with a clear beginning, middle, and ending.
- Use past simple consistently in a short story.
- Understand and use at least 100 useful expressions or sentence chunks, with a smaller core set becoming automatic.
- Respond to common casual questions without having to construct every sentence from scratch.
- Identify recurring grammar errors and correct many of them independently.
- Complete a listening exercise and summarize its main points.

# Part 2 — Your daily training system

Use the same basic structure each day. The topic and exercises change, but the learning process stays consistent.

## Morning session — 60 minutes

### 10 min

**1. Active recall**
Review yesterday's expressions without looking at their meanings. Produce an example sentence for each.

### 15 min

**2. Learn five useful chunks**
Learn the meaning, situation, pronunciation, and one example for each expression. Don't memorize isolated translations only.

### 15 min

**3. Grammar in context**
Study one small grammar point and correct 5–10 sentences that use it.

### 20 min

**4. Output practice**
Write or speak using the day's expressions. Produce your own sentences rather than copying the examples.

## Evening session — 60 minutes

### 15 min

**1. Listening**
Listen to a short, level-appropriate conversational clip. First understand the main idea; then listen for specific phrases.

### 15 min

**2. Shadowing**
Replay short sections and imitate the speaker's rhythm, stress, linking, and intonation.

### 20 min

**3. Speaking challenge**
Answer a question or role-play a situation using the day's expressions. Record yourself when possible.

### 10 min

**4. Error log**
Write down three mistakes, correct them, and say the corrected sentences aloud.

> [!important]
> Listening and shadowing are different. During listening, focus on comprehension. During shadowing, focus on reproducing what you hear.

# Part 3 — The detailed 28-day plan

## Week 1 — Build the foundation

### Theme: Everyday life and sentence construction

Days 1–7 · 14 hours

Your first priority is to stop producing fragments and begin producing complete sentences consistently. You'll practise talking about your routine, weekend, hobbies, and plans.

### Day 1 — Establish your baseline

**Morning — 60 minutes**

- 10 min: Write 10 sentences about your life without using a translator.
- 15 min: Learn these chunks:
    - "I usually spend my evenings..."
    - "I've been getting into..."
    - "I decided to try..."
    - "I ended up..."
    - "I'm planning to..."
- 15 min: Study the basic sentence pattern: subject + verb + object/complement.
- 20 min: Write 10 sentences about your typical day. Read them aloud.

**Evening — 60 minutes**

- 15 min: Listen to a beginner-friendly conversation about daily routines.
- 15 min: Shadow 5–8 short sentences.
- 20 min: Record a 60–90-second introduction about yourself, your job, hobbies, and typical weekend.
- 10 min: Note your five most noticeable mistakes.

**Deliverable:** your baseline recording and a list of recurring errors.

### Day 2 — Talk about your routine

**Morning**

- 10 min: Recall yesterday's five chunks.
- 15 min: Learn:
    - "I tend to..."
    - "Most of the time..."
    - "Once in a while..."
    - "I'm usually into..."
    - "I don't really feel like..."
- 15 min: Practise present simple, including third-person -s and questions with do/does.
- 20 min: Write 12 sentences about your weekday routine and weekend routine.

**Evening**

- 15 min: Listen to a conversation about someone's daily life.
- 15 min: Shadow sentences containing usually, sometimes, and tend to.
- 20 min: Speak for two minutes about how you spend a typical workday.
- 10 min: Correct three errors and repeat the relevant sentences.

**Deliverable:** a two-minute description of your routine.

### Day 3 — Describe your weekend

**Morning**

- 10 min: Review Days 1–2.
- 15 min: Learn:
    - "I went out with..."
    - "I stayed in..."
    - "I tried out..."
    - "I spent most of the day..."
    - "It was pretty chill."
- 15 min: Study past simple, especially regular and irregular verbs: played, tried, went, had, rode, felt.
- 20 min: Write 10 sentences about your previous weekend.

**Evening**

- 15 min: Listen to a short conversation about weekend activities.
- 15 min: Shadow five sentences and pay attention to past-tense endings.
- 20 min: Answer aloud: "What did you do last weekend?" Speak for 90 seconds without reading.
- 10 min: Check every completed action for correct past tense.

**Deliverable:** one natural-sounding weekend story.

### Day 4 — Talk about hobbies and interests

**Morning**

- 10 min: Recall the five Day 3 chunks.
- 15 min: Learn:
    - "I've recently taken up..."
    - "I'm really into..."
    - "I've been meaning to..."
    - "I'm still getting the hang of..."
    - "It's not really my thing."
- 15 min: Practise like doing, enjoy doing, want to do, and decide to do.
- 20 min: Write about learning guitar, gaming, exercise, or another hobby.

**Evening**

- 15 min: Listen to a conversation about someone's interests.
- 15 min: Shadow expressions used to explain preferences.
- 20 min: Speak for two minutes about one hobby, why you enjoy it, and what you want to improve.
- 10 min: Correct unnatural phrases.

**Deliverable:** a two-minute hobby introduction.

### Day 5 — Talk about plans

**Morning**

- 10 min: Review previously learned chunks.
- 15 min: Learn:
    - "I'm thinking of..."
    - "I'm hoping to..."
    - "I'm planning on..."
    - "I might..."
    - "We'll see how it goes."
- 15 min: Practise the differences between present continuous for arrangements, going to for intentions, and might for possibilities.
- 20 min: Write 10 sentences about your plans for the next week.

**Evening**

- 15 min: Listen to a conversation about making weekend plans.
- 15 min: Shadow questions and answers about availability.
- 20 min: Role-play arranging an activity with a friend. Include a change of plan.
- 10 min: Correct your future-tense sentences.

**Deliverable:** a realistic conversation about making plans.

### Day 6 — Connect sentences into a story

**Morning**

- 10 min: Review the past-tense verbs from Day 3.
- 15 min: Learn:
    - "At first..."
    - "Then..."
    - "After a while..."
    - "Eventually..."
    - "In the end..."
- 15 min: Practise connecting sentences with and, but, because, and so.
- 20 min: Write a 120–150-word story about a recent experience.

**Evening**

- 15 min: Listen to a short personal anecdote.
- 15 min: Shadow its transition phrases.
- 20 min: Retell your own story aloud without reading the complete text.
- 10 min: Identify places where your story jumps between ideas.

**Deliverable:** a connected story with a beginning, middle, and ending.

### Day 7 — Weekly review and test

**Morning**

- 10 min: Test yourself on 25 chunks from Days 1–6.
- 15 min: Review your five most frequent grammar errors.
- 15 min: Correct 10 sentences you wrote earlier.
- 20 min: Write 120 words about your weekend and your plans for next week.

**Evening**

- 15 min: Listen to a familiar conversation without subtitles, then summarize its main idea.
- 15 min: Repeat the clip with subtitles or a transcript and check what you missed.
- 20 min: Record a two-minute answer to "Tell me about yourself and what you enjoy doing."
- 10 min: Compare the recording with Day 1.

> [!check] Weekly checkpoint
> Can you produce complete sentences and describe your routine or weekend in connected English?

## Week 2 — Sound more natural in conversation

### Theme: Social English and spontaneous responses

Days 8–14 · 14 hours

This week is about reacting naturally. Instead of always giving short literal answers, you'll learn how to continue conversations, joke, express preferences, and ask follow-up questions.

### Day 8 — React naturally

**Morning**

- 10 min: Review 10 Week 1 expressions.
- 15 min: Learn:
    - "No way!"
    - "Seriously?"
    - "Fair enough."
    - "That makes sense."
    - "I get what you mean."
- 15 min: Practise short responses to statements and questions.
- 20 min: Write two possible responses to 10 everyday situations.

**Evening**

- 15 min: Listen to a conversation and identify how speakers react to one another.
- 15 min: Shadow short reactions, copying intonation.
- 20 min: Role-play a conversation about work, hobbies, and weekend plans.
- 10 min: Identify responses that sounded too formal or too short.

**Deliverable:** 10 natural reactions you can retrieve quickly.

### Day 9 — Agree and disagree

**Morning**

- 10 min: Review Day 8.
- 15 min: Learn:
    - "I see your point, but..."
    - "I'm not so sure about that."
    - "That depends."
    - "I see it differently."
    - "You've got a point."
- 15 min: Practise because, although, and however in simple opinions.
- 20 min: Write your opinions on five everyday topics and give a reason for each.

**Evening**

- 15 min: Listen to a discussion between two people.
- 15 min: Shadow expressions used to agree and disagree.
- 20 min: Speak for two minutes about a topic you have an opinion on. Include one opposing view.
- 10 min: Check whether each opinion has a clear reason.

**Deliverable:** a short, balanced opinion.

### Day 10 — Joke and tease a friend

**Morning**

- 10 min: Recall your previous social expressions.
- 15 min: Learn:
    - "You're kidding me!"
    - "You had one job!"
    - "I'm just messing with you."
    - "Happens to the best of us."
    - "I'll let you off this time."
- 15 min: Practise the difference between playful teasing and a genuinely angry response.
- 20 min: Write responses to five situations involving a friend forgetting something, arriving late, or making a small mistake.

**Evening**

- 15 min: Listen to casual dialogue with light humor.
- 15 min: Imitate the intonation, not just the words.
- 20 min: Role-play five scenarios. Give one friendly, one neutral, and one more direct response to each.
- 10 min: Review expressions that felt difficult to retrieve.

**Deliverable:** five playful responses you can use appropriately.

### Day 11 — Ask follow-up questions

**Morning**

- 10 min: Review social expressions.
- 15 min: Learn:
    - "How did that go?"
    - "What was that like?"
    - "How come?"
    - "What happened next?"
    - "What made you decide to do that?"
- 15 min: Practise open questions versus yes/no questions.
- 20 min: Write a conversation with at least eight follow-up questions.

**Evening**

- 15 min: Listen for questions in a casual conversation.
- 15 min: Shadow question intonation.
- 20 min: Practise keeping a conversation going for three minutes by asking relevant follow-up questions.
- 10 min: Check whether your questions relate to what the other person actually said.

**Deliverable:** a conversation that doesn't end after one question.

### Day 12 — Make plans and handle changes

**Morning**

- 10 min: Recall the expressions from Day 5.
- 15 min: Learn:
    - "Are you up for...?"
    - "I'm down for that."
    - "Something came up."
    - "Can we take a rain check?"
    - "Let's play it by ear."
- 15 min: Practise polite requests and suggestions using could, would, and do you want to.
- 20 min: Write three conversations: inviting a friend, declining an invitation, and changing plans.

**Evening**

- 15 min: Listen to a conversation about making arrangements.
- 15 min: Shadow the invitation and response phrases.
- 20 min: Role-play making plans when one person becomes unavailable.
- 10 min: Correct awkward or overly literal wording.

**Deliverable:** three short dialogues about real-life plans.

### Day 13 — Casual conversation at work

**Morning**

- 10 min: Review Days 8–12.
- 15 min: Learn:
    - "How's your week been?"
    - "Have you been up to much?"
    - "It's been pretty hectic."
    - "Can't complain."
    - "I'm looking forward to..."
- 15 min: Practise present perfect in common conversation, especially Have you...?, I've been..., and It's been...
- 20 min: Write a conversation with a coworker before or after work.

**Evening**

- 15 min: Listen to a casual workplace conversation.
- 15 min: Shadow the greeting, response, and follow-up.
- 20 min: Role-play a three-minute conversation with a coworker.
- 10 min: Review your use of questions and present perfect.

**Deliverable:** a relaxed workplace conversation.

### Day 14 — Weekly review and test

**Morning**

- 10 min: Recall 30 expressions from Week 2.
- 15 min: Review your weakest grammar point.
- 15 min: Correct 10 awkward responses from your practice.
- 20 min: Write a dialogue of 12–16 turns between two friends.

**Evening**

- 15 min: Listen to a new casual conversation without subtitles.
- 15 min: Listen again with the transcript.
- 20 min: Role-play a spontaneous conversation with five unexpected changes of topic.
- 10 min: Rate your responses for speed, clarity, and naturalness.

> [!check] Weekly checkpoint
> Can you react, ask follow-up questions, and maintain a conversation without relying on memorized full scripts?

## Week 3 — Tell stories and express emotions

### Theme: Narration, feelings, and reflection

Days 15–21 · 14 hours

Your Day 2 diagnostic showed that you want to communicate more than simple facts. This week teaches you to express what happened, how you felt, why you reacted, and what you learned.

### Day 15 — Describe events in sequence

**Morning**

- 10 min: Review past-tense verbs.
- 15 min: Learn:
    - "Out of nowhere..."
    - "It all started when..."
    - "A little later..."
    - "Before I knew it..."
    - "Looking back..."
- 15 min: Practise past simple and past continuous: I was walking when...
- 20 min: Write a 150-word story about something unexpected.

**Evening**

- 15 min: Listen to a personal anecdote.
- 15 min: Shadow the sequence markers.
- 20 min: Retell your story without reading it.
- 10 min: Check the chronological order and verb tenses.

**Deliverable:** a two-minute chronological story.

### Day 16 — Describe feelings precisely

**Morning**

- 10 min: Recall Day 15's expressions.
- 15 min: Learn:
    - "I felt overwhelmed."
    - "I was caught off guard."
    - "It left me feeling..."
    - "I wasn't sure how to process it."
    - "I needed some time to think."
- 15 min: Practise feel + adjective and make/leave + object + adjective or feeling phrase.
- 20 min: Write about three situations and describe the emotion each caused.

**Evening**

- 15 min: Listen to a personal story about a difficult experience.
- 15 min: Shadow the speaker's emotional phrases.
- 20 min: Describe an experience aloud, focusing on what you felt rather than only what happened.
- 10 min: Replace vague expressions like "I felt bad" with more precise language where appropriate.

**Deliverable:** three short descriptions of emotional experiences.

### Day 17 — Explain causes and consequences

**Morning**

- 10 min: Review emotional vocabulary.
- 15 min: Learn:
    - "The reason was that..."
    - "Because of that..."
    - "As a result..."
    - "That made me realize..."
    - "It turned out that..."
- 15 min: Practise because versus because of, and so versus as a result.
- 20 min: Write five cause-and-effect explanations about real or imagined events.

**Evening**

- 15 min: Listen to a story and identify the cause and consequence.
- 15 min: Shadow connecting phrases.
- 20 min: Explain a difficult situation using this structure: event → cause → consequence.
- 10 min: Check whether your explanation is logically connected.

**Deliverable:** one coherent cause-and-effect story.

### Day 18 — Talk about lessons and personal growth

**Morning**

- 10 min: Review Days 15–17.
- 15 min: Learn:
    - "It taught me that..."
    - "I came to realize that..."
    - "I used to think..., but now..."
    - "What I learned from it was..."
    - "If I could do it again..."
- 15 min: Practise used to, would, and if I could... in relevant contexts.
- 20 min: Write 150–200 words about a lesson you learned.

**Evening**

- 15 min: Listen to someone discussing a life lesson.
- 15 min: Shadow reflective expressions.
- 20 min: Give a two-minute answer to "What's something you've learned about yourself recently?"
- 10 min: Correct grammar and improve transitions.

**Deliverable:** a two-minute reflection.

### Day 19 — Talk about relationships and misunderstandings

**Morning**

- 10 min: Recall your emotional vocabulary.
- 15 min: Learn:
    - "We got our wires crossed."
    - "I didn't see it that way."
    - "I wish we'd talked about it sooner."
    - "I wasn't sure where we stood."
    - "I needed some clarity."
- 15 min: Practise reported speech and past-tense reporting: She told me that..., I thought we were...
- 20 min: Write a neutral account of a fictional misunderstanding between two people.

**Evening**

- 15 min: Listen to a dialogue about a misunderstanding.
- 15 min: Shadow the phrases used to explain different perspectives.
- 20 min: Retell the situation from both people's perspectives.
- 10 min: Check that you can distinguish facts from assumptions.

**Deliverable:** a balanced explanation of a misunderstanding.

### Day 20 — Explain abstract ideas simply

**Morning**

- 10 min: Review your most useful reflection phrases.
- 15 min: Learn:
    - "What I mean is..."
    - "In other words..."
    - "The way I see it..."
    - "It's not necessarily about..."
    - "There's a difference between..."
- 15 min: Practise defining a concept with a simple explanation and an example.
- 20 min: Explain an idea such as patience, freedom, integrity, or emotional maturity in 120–180 words.

**Evening**

- 15 min: Listen to a short discussion or explanation of an abstract concept.
- 15 min: Shadow the speaker's explanation structure.
- 20 min: Explain your chosen concept aloud, as if talking to a friend who has never studied philosophy.
- 10 min: Identify sentences that were too complicated or unclear.

**Deliverable:** a two-minute explanation of an abstract idea.

### Day 21 — Weekly review and storytelling test

**Morning**

- 10 min: Recall 30 expressions from Week 3.
- 15 min: Review past simple, past continuous, and reported speech.
- 15 min: Correct a previous story.
- 20 min: Write a 180–220-word story about a challenging experience and what you learned.

**Evening**

- 15 min: Listen to a new personal story.
- 15 min: Summarize the main event, emotional response, and outcome.
- 20 min: Record a three-minute personal story without reading a script.
- 10 min: Evaluate clarity, grammar, and transitions.

> [!check] Weekly checkpoint
> Can you tell a coherent story, explain your feelings, and reflect on an experience in connected English?

## Week 4 — Build fluency and automaticity

### Theme: Think faster, speak more naturally

Days 22–28 · 14 hours

This week combines everything you've learned. The focus shifts from studying expressions to retrieving them in conversation with less preparation.

### Day 22 — Reduce translation and hesitation

**Morning**

- 10 min: Review 20 difficult chunks.
- 15 min: Learn:
    - "Let me think for a second."
    - "How should I put it?"
    - "What I'm trying to say is..."
    - "I haven't really thought about it."
    - "That's a good question."
- 15 min: Practise answering without translating every sentence. Start with simple language.
- 20 min: Answer 10 everyday questions for 30–45 seconds each.

**Evening**

- 15 min: Listen to a natural conversation.
- 15 min: Shadow short responses with minimal pauses.
- 20 min: Do a rapid-response drill: answer 10 questions with no more than five seconds of preparation.
- 10 min: Record your longest pauses and note what caused them.

**Deliverable:** 10 spontaneous answers.

### Day 23 — Paraphrase when you don't know a word

**Morning**

- 10 min: Review yesterday's phrases.
- 15 min: Learn:
    - "It's a kind of..."
    - "It's something you use to..."
    - "It's similar to..."
    - "I can't remember the exact word, but..."
    - "What I mean is..."
- 15 min: Practise describing objects and ideas without naming them.
- 20 min: Describe 10 everyday objects in English without using their names.

**Evening**

- 15 min: Listen to a conversation containing explanations or paraphrases.
- 15 min: Shadow clarification phrases.
- 20 min: Play a guessing game: describe an object or situation so another person can identify it. If alone, record the clues and guess after a short pause.
- 10 min: Review how you handled missing vocabulary.

**Deliverable:** 10 descriptions without relying on the exact word.

### Day 24 — Handle unexpected questions

**Morning**

- 10 min: Recall your response phrases.
- 15 min: Learn:
    - "To be honest..."
    - "Off the top of my head..."
    - "It depends on the situation."
    - "I've never really looked at it that way."
    - "My first thought is..."
- 15 min: Practise giving an answer, a reason, and an example.
- 20 min: Answer eight unexpected questions about preferences, work, relationships, and daily life.

**Evening**

- 15 min: Listen to an interview or informal Q&A.
- 15 min: Shadow how speakers begin answers and expand their points.
- 20 min: Answer five random questions for 60 seconds each.
- 10 min: Check whether your answers contain a clear main point and supporting detail.

**Deliverable:** five extended answers with minimal preparation.

### Day 25 — Improve natural rhythm and pronunciation

**Morning**

- 10 min: Review the phrases you struggled to pronounce.
- 15 min: Learn five short conversational sentences from an audio source.
- 15 min: Practise word stress and sentence stress. Emphasize meaningful words instead of pronouncing every word equally.
- 20 min: Read the sentences aloud, then say them from memory.

**Evening**

- 15 min: Listen to a short conversation and identify stressed words.
- 15 min: Shadow the same 30–60 seconds several times.
- 20 min: Record yourself imitating the clip, then compare your rhythm and clarity with the original.
- 10 min: Choose two pronunciation issues to work on next.

**Deliverable:** a short before-and-after recording.

> [!note]
> Without hearing your voice, I cannot diagnose specific pronunciation errors. The aim here is to improve intelligibility and rhythm, not imitate an accent perfectly.

### Day 26 — Keep a conversation going

**Morning**

- 10 min: Review the most useful social expressions from Week 2.
- 15 min: Learn:
    - "What about you?"
    - "How did you get into that?"
    - "That reminds me of..."
    - "Speaking of..."
    - "Anyway, getting back to..."
- 15 min: Practise changing topics and returning to an earlier topic.
- 20 min: Write a conversation that includes a greeting, follow-up questions, a story, a reaction, and a topic change.

**Evening**

- 15 min: Listen to a longer casual conversation.
- 15 min: Shadow transitions and follow-up questions.
- 20 min: Hold a five-minute conversation with a partner or simulate one by recording both sides of a dialogue.
- 10 min: Evaluate whether you could maintain the interaction without reading a script.

**Deliverable:** a five-minute conversation.

### Day 27 — Full everyday English simulation

**Morning**

- 10 min: Review 30 core expressions.
- 15 min: Review your three most persistent grammar problems.
- 15 min: Prepare five conversational scenarios: weekend chat, making plans, joking with a friend, telling a story, and discussing an opinion.
- 20 min: Practise each scenario for two to three minutes.

**Evening**

- 15 min: Listen to an unfamiliar conversational clip without subtitles.
- 15 min: Listen again with a transcript and note useful phrases.
- 20 min: Record a conversation simulation that changes topics unexpectedly.
- 10 min: Review your errors without rewriting the entire conversation.

**Deliverable:** a realistic conversational performance.

### Day 28 — Final assessment

**Morning**

- 10 min: Review your error log.
- 15 min: Test yourself on 30 expressions selected from the four weeks.
- 15 min: Correct 10 sentences containing your most common mistakes.
- 20 min: Write a 180–220-word personal story without a translator.

**Evening**

- 15 min: Complete a new listening exercise without subtitles and summarize it.
- 15 min: Listen again with the transcript and evaluate comprehension.
- 20 min: Record three responses:
    1. "Tell me about yourself."
    2. "Tell me about something that didn't go as planned."
    3. "What is something you've learned recently?"
- 10 min: Compare the recordings with Day 1 and score your progress.

**Deliverable:** your final assessment portfolio: writing sample, recordings, expression test, and error log.

# Part 5 — How to study vocabulary so you remember it

Don't try to memorize every phrase in this plan equally. Use three levels.

**Level 1 — Recognition**

You understand the expression when you hear or read it.

**Level 2 — Controlled production**

You can use it when given a prompt or situation.

**Level 3 — Automatic retrieval**

You naturally use it in conversation without consciously recalling the lesson.

For each new expression, make a note in this format:

```
Expression: I ended up staying in.

Meaning: I eventually stayed at home.

Situation: I had planned to go out, but my plans changed.

Example: "I was going to meet some friends, but I ended up staying in."

My own sentence: I ended up ______.
```

Recall schedule: Same day → next day → 3 days later → 7 days later.

Five useful chunks learned deeply are more valuable than 20 expressions you recognize but cannot use.

# Part 6 — Weekly performance assessment

At the end of each week, score yourself from 0 to 5 in each category. Use evidence from your recordings and written work rather than judging yourself based on how confident you felt.

| Category | What to evaluate |
|---|---|
| Grammar | Can you form complete sentences and use the target tense accurately? |
| Vocabulary | Can you retrieve the expressions without looking at your notes? |
| Naturalness | Do your sentences sound appropriate for the situation? |
| Listening | Can you identify the main idea and key details? |
| Fluency | Can you continue speaking without excessive pauses? |
| Storytelling | Can you connect events, causes, feelings, and outcomes? |

A score of 0 means you could not complete the task; 3 means you can do it with some errors or hesitation; 5 means you can do it consistently and comfortably for that task.

# Part 7 — Rules that will make the plan work

1. **Speak aloud every day.** Silent study alone won't build spontaneous speaking ability.
2. **Use a transcript after your first listening attempt.** Don't immediately read subtitles and mistake recognition for listening comprehension.
3. **Correct recurring mistakes, not every mistake.** Each day, focus on your three most important errors.
4. **Reuse expressions across different situations.** If you learn "I ended up..." on Day 3, use it again on Days 6, 12, and 22.
5. **Keep the difficulty appropriate.** If you cannot understand the main idea of a listening clip after two attempts, choose an easier clip or a shorter segment.
6. **Do not use a translator before producing your first answer.** Try to express your idea independently, then compare your version with a corrected one.
7. **Repeat the same speaking task after correction.** First attempt → feedback → corrected attempt. That last repetition is essential.

For listening resources, use short A2–B1 everyday conversations with transcripts. BBC Learning English, British Council LearnEnglish, and VOA Learning English are reasonable places to find material. Choose the clip based on how well you understand it, not on the prestige of the source.

## Your daily mission

If you remember only one thing from this program, remember this cycle:

**Learn a useful expression → hear it in context → use it yourself → receive correction → use it again without looking.**

That is how you turn passive vocabulary into active conversational ability.

By Day 28, the most meaningful result won't necessarily be a new CEFR label. It will be whether you can describe your weekend, joke with a friend, tell a personal story, and express an opinion with less hesitation and fewer recurring mistakes than you did on Day 1.

**Four basic steps can guide your thinking.** With any decision, take time to:

Step 1: Generate questions

Ask yourself and others questions to help clarify your objectives, check for cognitive biases, and create space for new ideas.


Step 2: Consider options

Seek multiple options and evaluate them based on your objectives. Stay neutral as you analyze different approaches.


Step 3: Commit to a course of action and carry it out

Using insights from the first two steps, make your decision. Then switch your focus to successfully executing it.


Step 4: Reflect and iterate

After you implement the decision, take time to review it. Make adjustments or draw insights for future decisions.

## Step 1: Generate questions

- Clarify your objectives
    
    Clarify what you’re trying to achieve through the decision. 
    What problem do you need to solve? 
    What’s the ideal impact of the decision?
    
- Surface cognitive biases
    
    Analyze your perspective and emotions, checking for bias in your thinking. 
    Try to identify the factors that shape your approach to the issue. 
    Are there other ways to think about it?
    
- Make them open-ended
    
    Avoid yes/no questions. Instead, ask open-ended ones to help you explore the issue from different angles.

**Using open-ended questions will spark creative thinking and help you develop a set of strong alternatives to consider.**


## Consider the options


How effectively does each option meet the objectives?
How much risk or uncertainty does each option involve?
What consequences might each alternative have?

To probe and refine your thinking:

- Ask what you **haven’t** yet considered. When possible, assign someone the task of finding gaps in your thinking.
- Ensure all parties listen and respond to different viewpoints.
  
Create a pros and cons list

## Carry out the decision

In many situations, there may not be just one good decision. And if different stakeholders hold opposing views, you won’t be able to please everyone. **But don’t get stuck in endless debate.**

Decisions are influenced by:
- Human nature
- Complex situations
- Cultural expectations

## 1. Human Nature

- Habits
- Emotion
- Personal characteristics
- Unconscious bias

## 2. Complex Situations

- Risk
- Uncertainty
- Time pressure
- Multiple stakeholders

## 3. Cultural Expectations

People have different perspectives on how decisions should be made and what counts as a good decision. Organizations and teams develop their own cultures.

To start analyzing your expectations around decision making, you can ask questions:

- Who is in charge?
- What beliefs shape behavior at work?
- What concepts are valued, and why?

### Authority and Hierarchy

- Who has authority in your organization? Why?
- How important is it for your team members to respect the hierarchy? Why?
- What are the leader's main rules?

### Debate and Consensus

- Is debate highly valued? Why?
- How much is consensus emphasized? Why?
- How do groups reach consensus successfully?

### Efficiency

- Is speed important?
- What is the importance of speed compared to that of discussion and reflection?
- What do teams need to make decisions efficiently?

### Navigate ethical dilemmas

Stick to your values:
Identify one or two principles to guide you as you make tough choices.

Look for another way:
Take a step back and try to find or create a different set of options.

Get advice:
Talk through the issue with a trusted coworker or friend.

### Cultivate critical thinking

Keen observation

**Observe with curiosity and attention.** Notice different kinds of information such as patterns, details, ideas that are different or hard to grasp, and compelling words, images, symbols, or behaviors.


Thoughtful questioning

**Ask many questions in many ways.** As you observe and receive information, ask yourself and others open-ended questions that help you test ideas. Avoid rushing toward answers.


Reflection and analysis

**Think deeply** **about information, questions, pain points, and multiple perspectives.** Look for ways to categorize, evaluate, and rethink the input you receive.


Synthesis and judgment

**Bring together knowledge from different sources to make inferences and draw conclusions****.Make choices you can explain clearly to others, including those who may see things differently.

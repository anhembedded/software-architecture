## Barriers to good decisions

**Sometimes we fall into traps related to what we already know or have done**, such as:

Anchoring: Giving extra weight to the first thing you learn about an issue
Status quo bias: Tending to stick with how things already are
Sunk-cost trap: Overemphasizing time and resources you’ve invested

**Decisions can also be influenced by perceptions or emotions we’re not conscious of.** Learn to check yourself for these flaws in thinking:

Confirmation bias: Focusing on what you already think
Affinity bias: Connecting with and trusting those who are like you
Emotional attachment: Allowing positive feelings to cloud your thinking

## Counteract cognitive biases

  
Check your framing

Ask yourself questions about the decision itself and what’s shaping your thinking about it. By doing so, you’ll be more likely to recognize potential flaws in your framing or the assumptions that lie beneath the alternatives you’re considering.

Seek diverse input

Get opinions and advice from multiple sources, including people whose perspective is different from yours. This helps ensure that you have complete information and that you’re thinking about the decision from multiple angles.

Test your thinking

Actively look for new or contradictory information. Ask someone to take on the role of pushing back against ideas that are raised. Bringing in others to discuss the decision or challenge your thinking can help you identify points you wouldn’t notice on your own.

- **Define the Data:** Explicitly state what metrics, variables, and units are being presented. Clarify the exact scope of the numbers before analyzing them.
    
- **Assess the Source:** Evaluate the origin of the data. Identify the publisher, their potential biases, funding sources, and overall credibility.
    
- **Test for Accuracy:** Cross-examine the figures for mathematical consistency, statistical anomalies, and alignment with known baselines or external benchmarks.
    
- **Analyze Representation:** Determine if the sample accurately reflects the target population. Flag any sampling biases, overrepresented groups, or excluded demographics.
    
- **Challenge Assumptions:** Identify and dismantle underlying premises. Question whether the data's interpretation relies on unproven causations or logical leaps.
    
- **Hunt for Confounding Variables:** Actively search for hidden, external, or third-party factors that could explain the observed correlations or trends.
    
- **Evaluate Collection Method:** Scrutinize the methodology used to gather the data (e.g., self-reported surveys, automated telemetry, manual entry) and highlight inherent flaws in that approach.
    
- **Consider Context:** Place the numbers within their broader environmental, historical, or economic background. Do not analyze the data in a vacuum.
    
- **Know Limitations:** Explicitly state what this data _cannot_ prove. Outline margins of error, blind spots, and what additional information is required to reach a definitive conclusion.
